01

Tax consulting in two minutes

Tax consulting isn't just filing returns. Teams like Deloitte Tax help companies stay compliant, plan legally around tax, and run their tax function better, with the right people, processes, and technology. This case is about that last part: tax operations.

You don't need deep tax law for this case. It practices structuring a business problem with a tax setting: cost, risk, and scale. The basics you need are below.

Sales tax basics

What the client is dealing with

  • A retailer collects sales tax from customers and sends it to each state.
  • Every state has its own rates, its own rules on what's taxable, and its own filing schedule.
  • Since the Supreme Court's 2018 South Dakota v. Wayfair decision, states can require online sellers to collect tax once their sales in the state pass a threshold, even without a store there.
  • Late or wrong returns bring penalties, interest, and notices.

What this page adds

Practice tools

Ten terms to drop naturally

Indirect tax

Taxes on transactions, like sales tax, as opposed to income tax.

Nexus

Enough connection to a state that you must collect and file its tax.

Economic nexus

Nexus created by sales volume into a state, not a physical presence. The result of Wayfair.

Taxability

Whether a product is taxed in a given state. Some states exempt certain clothing or food, for example.

Filing frequency

How often a state wants returns: monthly, quarterly, or yearly, usually based on how much tax you collect there.

Notice

A letter from a state about a missing, late, or wrong return. Each one takes time to resolve.

Tax engine

Software that calculates the right tax on each sale and prepares and files returns.

Managed services

Outsourcing a process, like sales tax filing, to a provider that runs it for a fee.

Voluntary disclosure agreement

Coming forward to a state about tax you should have collected in the past, usually in exchange for reduced penalties.

FTE

Full-time equivalent: about 1,800 working hours a year. A way to turn hours into headcount.

The case prompt

Interviewer

Brightwater Outdoor sells outdoor gear online, with about 300 million dollars in sales, growing 25 percent a year. It now collects sales tax in 28 states. Its three-person tax team files every return by hand, using spreadsheets.

Your move

Restate the question in one sentence before you do anything else.

Strong answer

"So the CFO wants to choose the best way to handle sales tax compliance, in-house, software, or outsourced, based on cost, accuracy, and whether it can scale from 28 to 40 states. Is that right?"

Interviewer: "Yes." Naming the three criteria, cost, accuracy, and scale, gives you a scorecard for the whole case.

03

Clarifying questions

Your move

Ask two or three clarifying questions. Pick ones whose answers would change your recommendation.

Strong questions, with the interviewer's answers

  • What matters most to the CFO: cost, accuracy, or something else?
    Accuracy first, since penalties and notices worry the board. Then cost. She'd rather not add headcount.
  • How quickly does a solution need to be in place?
    Within about 6 months, before the new states start.
  • Where does the sales data come from?
    An e-commerce platform. The team exports it to spreadsheets each month.
  • Were there states where Brightwater should have registered earlier?
    Yes, the team thinks it registered late in a few states.

The last question is the tax consultant's instinct: late registration can mean past tax that was never collected. That's a separate risk to fix, often through voluntary disclosure.

04

Build the framework

Interviewer

How would you approach the CFO's question?

Your move

Take 60 seconds. Diagnose today, compare the options, then cover implementation and risk.

Strong framework

Should Brightwater keep sales tax in-house, buy software, or outsource?
1Today
  • Returns, hours, and cost
  • Errors, penalties, notices
  • Root causes
2Options
  • Cost now and at 40 states
  • Accuracy and risk
  • Control and scalability
3Implementation
  • Data cleanup and integration
  • Team skills and change
  • 6-month timeline
4Past exposure
  • Late registrations
  • Uncollected tax
  • Voluntary disclosure

Say it like this

"I'd look at four things. First, today: how many returns, how many hours, and what's causing the errors and penalties. Second, the options: cost this year and at 40 states, plus accuracy, control, and how well each scales. Third, implementation: what each option needs in data, systems, and people within 6 months. Fourth, past exposure: if Brightwater registered late anywhere, it may owe tax it never collected, which needs fixing whichever option we pick. I'd start with today's costs."

Why it works

  • It compares options on the CFO's criteria, not just price.
  • It looks one year ahead, because the client is growing fast.
  • The fourth branch shows tax judgment beyond the question that was asked.

Exhibit 1: what it costs today

Interviewer

Brightwater files monthly in 16 states and quarterly in 12. Here's how long each return takes by hand. The team's time costs about 70 dollars an hour, fully loaded. What does sales tax compliance cost today?

Your move

Count the returns first. Then hours, labor cost, and penalties.

Quick check: the CFO suggests hiring one more tax analyst. Why isn't that the full answer?

Labor is only about 30 percent of today's cost. A manual process with more people still makes rate and taxability errors, and the volume grows by almost half next year.

07

Does the answer hold at 40 states?

Interviewer

Next year Brightwater will file about 350 returns in 40 states. The current team is at capacity, so staying in-house means hiring one more person at about 100 thousand dollars. Software moves to a 120 thousand dollar license tier, and the managed service charges 340 thousand above 300 returns. What's the picture next year?

Your move

Redo each option at 350 returns. Keep the same hours per return and penalty rates.

Hint: penalties scale with returns

Today's penalty rate is about 1 thousand dollars per return. At 350 returns, staying in-house means about 350 thousand of penalties. Software cuts that by 80 percent, the managed service by 90.

  1. 1 · Keep in-house$147K + $100K hire + $350K penalties≈$597K
  2. 2 · Tax software$120K + $37K + $70K penalties≈$227K
  3. 3 · Managed service$340K + $20K review + $35K penalties≈$395K

08

Score the implementation risks

Interviewer

Say the CFO picks software. Here are six risks to the rollout. Score each for likelihood and impact. Which three would you manage first?

Your move

Score each risk with a one-line reason, then name your top three and how you'd handle each.

The team's scores, with reasons

    Say it like this

    "First, messy product data: software is only as accurate as the taxability codes behind each product, so we clean those before go-live. Second, past exposure from late registrations: we review it now and use voluntary disclosure where it makes sense, because automation doesn't fix the past. Third, integration with the e-commerce platform: it's the critical path for the 6-month deadline, so we start it first and keep the manual process running until the new one is tested."

    09

    The recommendation

    Interviewer

    The CFO has 2 minutes before her board meeting. What do you tell her?

    Your move

    Lead with the answer. Then the numbers, the main risk, and next steps.

    Good answer

    "Buy the tax software. It costs about 169 thousand a year against 341 thousand today, and it pays back its setup cost in about 4 months."

    Better answer

    "Buy the tax software, and fix the past at the same time. Today's problem is accuracy: 70 percent of the cost is penalties. Software cuts the total from about 341 thousand to about 169 thousand this year, and next year, at 40 states, it costs about 227 thousand against nearly 600 thousand for staying in-house, with no new hire. It pays back in about 4 months."

    "The managed service is accurate but costs about 170 thousand more a year at 40 states, and it takes control away from the team."

    "Two things to get right: clean the product taxability data before go-live, and review the states where Brightwater registered late, using voluntary disclosure to limit penalties. Next steps: pick a vendor within 30 days, start the platform integration, and run both processes side by side for one filing cycle."

    The good answer is right on cost. The better answer adds scale, the past-exposure issue a tax professional would catch, and a safe way to switch over.

    10

    Bonus drills: tax math

    Quick reps

    1. A customer buys an 80 dollar jacket in a state with a 7.25 percent sales tax. How much tax?

    "5.80 dollars. 7 percent is 5.60 and 0.25 percent is 0.20."

    2. A company has 40 million of pre-tax income and 10 million of income tax expense. What's its effective tax rate?

    "25 percent. Tax expense divided by pre-tax income."

    3. A process takes 2,700 hours a year. About how many full-time people is that?

    "About 1.5. 2,700 divided by 1,800 hours a year."

    4. Each return has a 5 percent chance of a 4 thousand dollar penalty. What's the expected penalty on 300 returns?

    "60 thousand dollars. 300 times 5 percent is 15 penalties, times 4 thousand."
